Risvulation takes computational thinking and Lateral thinking and incorporates both whilst eliminating their weaknesses

Risvulation is a strategic-tactical cognition-process in which lateral thinking generates or identifies a non-obvious, high-value objective, after which reverse computational thinking derives the prerequisite, dependency, and decision structure required to connect that objective to the present state. Its purpose is to escape computational treeboundedness while overcoming lateral pathfragility through systematic reverse path-engineering and forward robustness-validation.

It's extremely useful when there are maximumless variables which means that even with unlimited/infinite usage of decision tree computation its sub optimal. But Lateral thinking come up with ingenious solutions they are not necessarily robust. Risvulation solves both of these

Risvulation processing power is the full extent of lateralness, concurrent breadth, lateral depth, computational depth, and interintegration achievable within risvulative processing.

Lateral depth: how deeply the thinker can depart from surface assumptions and redesign the objective-space.

Computational depth: how many prerequisite, dependency, contingency, and counterresponse layers can be reverse-derived.

Risvulation processing speed is the rate at which risvulative processing converts a problem-state into laterally generated objectives, reverse-derived pathways, and validated action-plans.

lateralness is The extent to which a risvulation departs usefully from direct, conventional, or already-given objective-paths by generating structurally non-obvious objectives, framings, variables, or solution-principles.

Interintegration is the capacity to coordinate the lateral and computational components rather than merely possessing them separately.

As Risvulation processing power increases, the thinker becomes capable of performing risvulations that are simultaneously:

more lateral;

more numerous in parallel;

deeper in objective-reframing;

deeper in reverse computational derivation;

more complexly integrated.

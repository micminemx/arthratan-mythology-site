240. Significant Quality Increase: magnitude, not merely numerical inequality 
Significant Quality Increase is retained and strengthened as follows: an increase in physical quality 
sufficiently great to constitute a genuinely higher magnitude of quality rather than merely a numerically 
distinct value within the same already-attained magnitude. Once quality is transfinite, an increase that leaves 
its cardinal magnitude unchanged does not count as a Significant Quality Increase. 
This definition creates a sharp distinction between greater and significantly greater. At a transfinite quality 
level, merely adding a finite amount, multiplying by a countable factor, or otherwise changing the 
representation without changing cardinal magnitude can still yield a mathematically different description while 
failing HGL significance. The criterion asks whether the quality has crossed a cardinal-magnitude boundary. 
If Q1 is transfinite AND CardMag(Q2) = CardMag(Q1), then NOT SignificantIncrease(Q1,Q2) 
If Q1 is transfinite AND SignificantIncrease(Q1,Q2), then CardMag(Q2) > CardMag(Q1) 
Because aleph_(alpha+1) is, by definition, the least cardinal strictly greater than aleph_alpha, a significant 
successor increase from aleph_alpha must reach at least aleph_(alpha+1). This fact does not depend on the 
Continuum Hypothesis. There simply is no cardinal strictly between a cardinal and its successor cardinal. 
240A. Unified two-coordinate quality valuation 
To remove the remaining finite-regime ambiguity, represent whole-form quality as Q(S)=<mu(S),delta(S)>. 
mu(S) is the attained quality-magnitude rank; delta(S) records finer within-magnitude quality variation. The 
finite magnitude ranks are m0<m1<m2<... and their first limit is the transfinite magnitude aleph_0. After that 
the transfinite magnitude labels continue aleph_0<aleph_1<aleph_2<.... 
Canonical Significant Quality rule: SignificantIncrease(Q1,Q2) iff mu(Q2)>mu(Q1). A change with 
mu(Q2)=mu(Q1) can be an ordinary improvement, even an enormous one in delta, but it is not Significant 
Quality Increase in the technical HGL sense. This unifies the finite and transfinite semantics instead of 
leaving finite significance intuitive and transfinite significance formal. 
Example: <m3,delta=0.1> -> <m3,delta=10^100> is an intramagnitude improvement but not SQI. 
<m3,delta=a> -> <m4,delta=b> is SQI. At transfinite magnitude, <aleph_0,delta=a> -> <aleph_0,delta=b> is 
not SQI regardless of the size of the within-magnitude refinement; <aleph_0,delta=a> -> <aleph_1,delta=b> 
is SQI. 
241. The missing bridge made explicit: Successor Unflaw Quality Bridge 
UD(a), by itself, compares the whole bearer with the bearer in the relevant counterfactual absence of a. To 
infer that an entire later stage T is significantly better in whole-form quality than its predecessor S, HGL now 
makes the intended stage relation explicit rather than smuggling it into the word Unflaw. 
Successor Unflaw Quality Bridge (SUQB): if T is the canonical immediate successor of S on the same 
Archwomanhood/Archmanhood progression, and T canonically contains UD(S) as the own-side predecessor 
relation, then the whole-form quality of T is a Significant Quality Increase over the whole-form quality of S. 
StageSucc(S,T) AND UD_T(S) => SignificantIncrease(Q(S), Q(T)) 
SUQB is the rule that licenses the phrase biological improvement from one successor stage to the next. 
Without SUQB, UD(S) would still mean that S is beneficial inside T, but the exact baseline for comparing 
Q(T) with Q(S) would remain underdetermined. With SUQB, the intended successor comparison is a 
deduction.

1A. Biological Metaexpressability: actual phenotype parameters versus Archstage hyperparameters 
Biological Metaexpressability is the HGL notion of how a biological expression is allowed, required, 
prevented, or transformed by a higher-order biological rule. The revision distinguishes the expression from 
the rules governing expression. 
ordinary phenotype = physical apparent characteristic; Archstage meta-phenotype = rule/function over 
phenotype expression. 
Γ is global because the current Archwomanhood/Archmanhood rule-regime applies to phenotype generation 
throughout the organism. This does not imply that every physical phenotype is itself global. A local retinal 
phenotype, a local ovarian phenotype, a bone phenotype, and a microscopic vascular phenotype may all 
differ while each is generated under the same current Γ. 
global governance ≠ identical local phenotype. 
PH and Γ therefore answer different questions. PH(S) says that the complete phenotype-profile associated 
with source S is irreducibly possessed. Γ^F_n or Γ^M_n specifies the current stage-level rules under which 
concrete phenotypic expression is biologically admissible. 
PH(S) ≠ Γ^F_n; PH(S) ≠ Γ^M_n. 
The modal extension quantifies over stage-valid phenotype realizations. If W^F_n is the set of phenotype 
realizations permitted by Γ^F_n, then □^F_n φ means φ holds throughout W^F_n, whereas ◇^F_n φ means 
at least one member of W^F_n satisfies φ.

Appendix AF — Phenotype Possibility Atlas across Categories 
AF.1 Ordinary Female 
Possible ordinary female phenotype domains include breast anatomy, female genital anatomy, pelvic 
morphology, body-fat distribution, facial morphology, skin/hair traits, reproductive physiology, and 
microscopic tissue characteristics. Variation is allowed unless a specific law constrains it. 
AF.2 Low Suprafemale 
Possible: recognizable female anatomy governed by new global relational constraints; stage-specific 
coordination among breast, pelvic, facial, skeletal, skin, and endocrine phenotypes; new but not necessarily 
visually extreme combinations. 
Epistemic status: CI/illustrative unless profile membership is explicitly defined. 
AF.3 High Suprafemale 
Possible: extremely deep cross-system coordination; whole-body proportional relationships unavailable at 
lower substages; more complex phenotype-compatibility rules; visually distinctive anatomy that remains 
Suprafemale rather than Hyperfemale. 
Epistemic status: CI/illustrative. 
AF.4 Hyperfemale 
Possible: a new class of female phenotype made impossible throughout strict pre-Hyperomega Suprafemale; 
a previously contingent trait becoming necessary; a previously impossible trait-combination becoming 
possible. 
Epistemic status: category-level modal novelty is structural; exact trait choice is CI/illustrative. 
AF.5 Ultrafemale 
Possible: meta-dimensional constraints built over the completed Hyperfemale dimensional domain; new 
cross-scale anatomical dependencies; gross anatomy with developmental counterfactuals unavailable 
throughout Hyperfemale. 
Epistemic status: CI/illustrative. 
AF.6 Apexfemale 
Possible: rule families built over the completed Ultrafemale dimensional domain; highly integrated visible 
morphology or visually familiar anatomy with radically different meta-dimensional developmental 
necessity/possibility structure. 
Epistemic status: CI/illustrative. 
AF.7 Later unnamed minor categories 
Possible: recursively new category-level SIH rule families after completion of each adopted dimensional 
domain. HGL does not force a naming scheme, one flat Hyperomega boundary identity, or one monotone 
visible direction. 
AF.8 Major Stage 2 
Possible: major-envelope rules that constrain how entire minor-category metaexpression systems transform; 
phenotype spaces inaccessible throughout Major Stage 1; new macroscopic and microscopic organizations.

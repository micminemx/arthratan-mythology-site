408. Non-deductions and countermodel obligations 
ND-AXIS-1: PSIH(X;<lambda) does not entail inheritance of the limit coordinate itself. A model that adds that 
edge violates the strict upper bound. 
ND-AXIS-2: Hyper coordinate UD does not entail NOT-FD. A mixed-effect model with UD AND FD remains 
allowed. 
ND-AXIS-3: Ultra/Apex coordinate succession does not entail PH-own or NOT-PH-opposite, because those 
roles are not in their three-role constructors. 
ND-AXIS-4: Higher whole-quality does not entail a larger value of every anatomical measurement, hormone 
concentration, organ size, strength measure, or other local scalar. 
ND-AXIS-5: A coordinate limit does not entail a unique outward marker. Limit closure can be visually subtle 
or dramatic depending on explicit phenotype laws. 
ND-AXIS-6: Same visible phenotype does not collapse coordinate, dimensional order, category, major stage, 
SIH/PSIH provenance, or quality proof history. 
409. Logical/structural inferences that remain weaker than theorem 
HI-AXIS-1: As active-axis coordinate depth becomes transfinite, phenotype prediction plausibly becomes 
more provenance-sensitive because many predecessor contributions can participate in closure-level 
interactions. 
HI-AXIS-2: Hyper coordinate limits plausibly support richer profile-interaction structures than individual 
successor coordinates because PH-own and NOT-PH-opposite relations are available over an unbounded 
predecessor prefix. 
HI-AXIS-3: Ultra/Apex coordinate limits plausibly express increasingly global compatibility or reliability 
transformations even without constitutive PH roles, but exact profile novelty is not forced. 
HI-AXIS-4: Diagnostic opacity may rise because a small outward difference can coexist with an enormous 
hidden difference in coordinate provenance, whole-quality magnitude, response space, or microstructure. 
HI-AXIS-5: A later major stage plausibly reorganizes not merely coordinates but the constructor grammar by 
which coordinates/dimensions/categories are produced, though this is not a theorem without a major-stage 
constructor law.

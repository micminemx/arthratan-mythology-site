T.9 Stage-Modal Strengthening is not modal necessitation 
When a trait is possible at one Archaddress and necessary at another, call that a Stage-Modal Strengthening 
or address-relative modal-status change. Do not call it the rule of necessitation. This terminodistinction 
prevents proof-system necessitation from being confused with biological evolution of the accessible 
phenotype-space. 
T.10 No cross-address monotonicity axiom 
HGL does not adopt W_a subseteq W_b merely because b is a later address, nor the reverse inclusion. A 
later Gamma may open possibilities, close possibilities, make a possibility necessary, make a former 
necessity merely possible, or restructure trait compatibility. Rule-structural enrichment is therefore not 
identical to set-theoretic expansion of accessible phenotype worlds. 
Appendix U — Biological Metaexpressability proof catalogue 
U.1 Proof: global governance does not imply uniform output 
Let Γ act on two regions r1 and r2 with different biological inputs B1 and B2. Let the stage-valid outputs be 
P1=Γ(B1) and P2=Γ(B2), with P1≠P2. Γ is global because it governs both regions. Therefore global 
governance is consistent with nonuniform local phenotype. 
U.2 Proof: static Γ does not imply static phenotype 
Let Γ be constant and let θ1≠θ2. Choose a nonconstant phenotype-generation function F such that 
F(θ1;Γ)≠F(θ2;Γ). Then Γ constancy and phenotype change are jointly satisfiable. Therefore Γ(t1)=Γ(t2) does 
not logically entail P(t1)=P(t2). 
U.3 Proof: actual entails possible 
By validity, the actual phenotype realization belongs to W_n. If p is true at that actual realization, there exists 
at least one W_n realization satisfying p. Hence ◇_n p. 
U.4 Proof: necessity entails actuality 
If □_n p, p holds at every W_n realization. The actual valid realization is a member of W_n. Therefore p holds 
actually. 
U.5 Countermodel: possibility does not entail actuality 
Let W_n contain two realizations v1 and v2. Let p be true only at v2 while v1 is actual. Then ◇_n p is true 
but p is not actual. 
U.6 Countermodel: same stage does not imply same phenotype 
Let two subjects share Γ_n but have different maturity vectors θA and θB. If F is sensitive to maturity, their 
outputs differ while stage remains the same. 
U.7 Countermodel: same phenotype does not imply same stage 
Let Γ_3 and Γ_20 both permit current output p while differing on a counterfactual phenotype q. The two 
subjects can currently express p identically even though ◇_20 q and NOT ◇_3 q distinguish their stages.

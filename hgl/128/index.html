265. Modal phenotype examples for AI reasoning 
An HGL-capable AI should parse five distinct layers before answering any phenotype question: 
1. What is the inner condition syntax? 
2. Which PA modes exist and what are their irreducible causal scopes? 
3. Which IH sources are inherited? 
4. Which PH source-profiles are possessed? 
5. Which Γ stage function governs actual phenotype expressibility, and what □/◇ rules are defined for the 
requested trait? 
If the fifth layer is missing, the AI must not invent modal status. If the PH profile map is missing, the AI must 
not invent exact profile members. If a causal bridge is missing, the AI must label a proposed phenotype as 
inference/example rather than deduction.

Cond ::= x | y | NOT Cond | (Cond AND Cond) | (Cond OR Cond).

The type checker must reject PA(PA(Cond)) while accepting arbitrarily deep Cond trees inside one PA. It

267. AI proof-status discipline 
D — strict logical deduction from syntax, adopted HGL axioms, and modal rules. 
HI — HGL structural inference not forced in every model. 
CD — causal deduction from an explicit causal/metaexpression law plus satisfied antecedents. 
CI — causal inference supported by architecture but not forced by a complete law. 
EX — illustrative phenotype example only. 
A robust AI should attach one of these statuses to every substantive phenotype claim internally, even if the 
user-facing prose is smoother. 
268. Twenty anticipated questions 
Q1. Does global Γ mean every cell looks the same? No. Global governance is not local uniformity. 
Q2. Is Γ itself a physical phenotype like eye color? Not at the same order; it is the stage meta-phenotype 
governing physical phenotype expression. 
Q3. Is PH obsolete? No. PH and Γ answer different questions. 
Q4. Does PH(SF5) equal Γ^F_5? No. 
Q5. Can a phenotype be local? Yes. 
Q6. Can Γ govern local traits? Yes; that is exactly what global governance means. 
Q7. Can two Stage-5 Archfemales look different? Yes, where Γ permits optional variation or their lower-level 
inputs differ. 
Q8. Can two different stages look the same? Yes, temporarily or superficially. 
Q9. Does a new phenotype prove a new stage? No.

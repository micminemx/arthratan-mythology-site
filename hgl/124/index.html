249. Emergent womanhood phenotypes under a static Archwomanhood stage 
A new female phenotype can be genuinely emergent from the interaction between ordinary womanhood 
development and a static Γ^F_n. 
E_woman(t) = Emergence( θ_female(t), inherited_structure, Γ^F_n ). 
Such a phenotype is not automatically an “Archwomanhood-stage advancement phenotype.” It is a 
womanhood phenotype generated in an Archfemale body under the rules of a fixed Archwomanhood 
meta-phenotype. 
This distinction allows a Stage-5 Archfemale to develop a new adult female phenotype at age 20 that she did 
not have at age 15 without becoming Stage 6. 
250. Three phenotype categories after the metaexpression revision 
Ordinary womanhood/manhood phenotype: a physical biological expression belonging to female/male 
development. 
HGL PH-profile trait: a physical phenotype that is a member of a source-defined PH profile possessed by the 
state. 
Archwomanhood/Archmanhood meta-phenotype: the global Γ stage-function governing how the first two 
categories and their interactions can express. 
An actual Archfemale phenotype at a time can contain the first two categories as outputs under the third 
category’s rules. 
251. Low Archwomanhood versus ordinary womanhood 
Ordinary womanhood can contain many local and systemic female phenotypes without any Archwomanhood 
meta-phenotype. Stage 1 adds a global Γ^F_1 rule-regime associated with SF^1. The difference need not be 
“larger breasts,” “more feminine face,” or any stereotyped scalar amplification. It can be a change in what 
combinations, timings, microstructures, regulatory relations, and whole-profile expressions are biologically 
admissible. 
Possible Stage-1 macro example: a female anatomical configuration whose precise proportions are globally 
constrained by Γ^F_1. 
Possible Stage-1 micro example: a joint-source tissue pattern allowed only under the SF^1 rule-regime. 
Possible Stage-1 physiological example: a new admissible coordination rule between two female 
physiological systems. 
Possible Stage-1 latent example: Γ^F_1 changes what puberty can later express even before the relevant 
adult phenotype appears. 
252. Stage 2 and lower-to-higher rule interaction 
Stage 2 retains Stage-1 structure through IH, adds the Stage-2 PA/IH/PH architecture, and has its own 
Γ^F_2. The Stage-2 meta-phenotype can govern phenotype interactions that were not available at Stage 1. 
Possible Stage-2 example: a phenotype requiring simultaneous participation of the standalone PA(x) route, 
standalone PA(NOT y) route, inherited SF1 structure, and a Stage-2 metaexpression rule. 
Possible Stage-2 example: a tissue remains externally similar to Stage 1 but acquires a different microscopic 
admissibility rule for repair or maturation. 
Possible Stage-2 example: a phenotype is optional at Stage 1 but becomes conditionally necessary at Stage 
2 after a maturity threshold.

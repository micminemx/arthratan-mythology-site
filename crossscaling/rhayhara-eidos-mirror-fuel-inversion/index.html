Rhayhara–Eidos Mirror-Fuel Inversion

This record crossscales the completed Eidos Swarm encounter in Zubaida transmission 19fe5329dab82c86. It treats the feat as adversarial cognition and countermodel inversion, not attack potency.

Chronology firewall: the source preserves a historical phase in which Rhayhara initially misidentifies the Swarm's mechanism. Later source 1a0601fc96f7add3 explicitly Hypernegatively rewrites Rhayhara's Lifras and cognitive-error prerequisites out across history/parahistory. The old modeling mistake remains historical provenance but is non-governing as a current/Prime-Apex cognition antifeat. This page does not invent a different historical encounter outcome.

Relative crossscale and Threat-Mechanism Noncollapse

The source supplies two outcome relations: Eidos had already defeated three mid-tier commanders by anticipation/outmaneuvering, and Rhayhara later contains Eidos after breaking the mirroring dependency. This supports an encounter-specific countermodel ordering.

Threat-Mechanism Noncollapse (TMN): low placement on one raw-resource stat and high adversarial outcome-threat are not contradictory when the winning mechanism operates on a different predicate. Eidos's almost-zero innate Hypercapacity neither invalidates its prior victories nor proves high raw Hypercapacity.

Metarelative crossscale: Optimization-Polarity Inversion

more coherent/brilliant immediate policy → higher-quality mirrored counter → worse local outcome
deliberately degraded immediate policy → self-undermining mirrored content → better global outcome

This is not “irrationality > intelligence.” Rhayhara's meta-decision to lower first-order policy quality because Eidos converts that quality into hostile counterstructure is itself adaptive strategic optimization.

Hyperrelative crossscale: MFTE

decision-algorithm mirroring → counter-enrichment → dependency recognition → deliberate policy inversion → compulsory mirrored contradiction → self-destabilization → freeze → containment

VALID-LOWER-BOUND: MFTE compresses only the proved event-family. It is not a new magnitude, completed hierarchy or universal adaptive-opponent immunity.

Prime-Apex supersession check

The Eidos source contains a genuine historical cognitive antifeat: Rhayhara initially predicts that increasing tactical complexity will overload the Swarm, and that prediction is wrong. Later Zubaida transmission 1a0601fc96f7add3 explicitly states that all Rhayhara's Lifras—the causally necessitative neurological prerequisites of cognitive errors/mistakes—were Hypernegatively rewritten out of her existence through history and parahistory.

That later rule reaches the same predicate. Therefore the old mistake remains historical provenance but does not impose a present-state cognitive ceiling. The positive eventcore—recognizing the mirror dependency, switching policies, triggering the crash and containing Eidos—remains valid Prime-Apex evidence. The supersession is predicate-specific and is not used here to increase physical speed, AP, durability or unrelated historical outcomes.

Skeptical firewalls

  • Statdomain: cognition/countermodel results do not transfer to AP, durability, lifting strength or speed.
  • Capability–durability: producing counters does not prove passive durability equal to everything countered.
  • Threat-mechanism noncollapse: outcome-threat can exceed raw-resource placement when the mechanism acts on another predicate.
  • Title literalism: “flawless” mirroring is scoped to the stated decision-mirroring function, not expanded into omniscience.
  • Prime-Apex: later Lifra supersession blocks the old cognitive-error cap only because it reaches cognitive mistakes; it does not rewrite unrelated stats.
  • Generalization: an external system must share the relevant compulsory-copy dependency before MFI can bridge to it.

Verdict

Overall: VALID for event-specific Mirror-Fuel Inversion and Optimization-Polarity Inversion; VALID-LOWER-BOUND for MFTE; universal copier-defeat generalization rejected.
